From radio to map

Every recorded feed runs through the same line. Each step either drops the clip, which costs nothing further, or passes it on:

feed → 45 s segments → silence gate → adaptive squelch → speech-to-text → filters → speech gate → channel story + triage → geocode → map · log · captions · alerts

Listen-only entries on the dial (LiveATC, NOAA Weather Radio) aren't recorded; tuning one opens its own page.

Capture and squelch

  • Segments. ffmpeg cuts each Broadcastify stream into 45-second pieces (SEGMENT_SECONDS).
  • Silence gate. ffmpeg's silenceremove drops everything quieter than −42 dB (per feed: squelchDb) and gaps longer than 0.7 s. A piece left with under 1.5 s of sound is dropped.
  • Adaptive squelch. Hum, hiss and an open carrier are loud enough to pass the silence gate, so a second check listens for speech. The clip is split into 32 ms frames and 16 bands from 300 to 3,400 Hz. Each band's background is measured from the clip itself (its 30th percentile). A frame counts as speech when at least 4 bands are 6 dB or more above their background.
    • Under 1.0 s of speech: dropped before it reaches the transcriber.
    • Under 85% speech: the non-speech parts are cut out.
    This is what keeps a channel like Greene County Fire, which hisses all day, from ever producing a clip.
  • Retention. Clips are kept for 7 days (RECORD_DAYS), longer if a daily log uses them. Transcripts, incident updates and stories are kept for 30 days; daily logs stay.

Transcription and the speech gate

  • Speech-to-text. Each clip goes to google/gemini-3.8-flash through OpenRouter, with the area's place and street names as vocabulary.
  • Filters. Transcribers sometimes invent words from noise. Empty answers, placeholders, the model's own commentary ("no intelligible speech") and echoes of the prompt are thrown out.
  • Speech gate. TypeSafe's Jev asks two quick questions of every transcript: is this real speech, and is it newsworthy?
    • Speech under 0.3: the clip is dropped as filler.
    • Newsworthy under 0.2: the clip is kept but skips incident triage. 5% of skipped clips are triaged anyway, to catch misses.

Incidents

Triage (openai/gpt-6-luna, strict JSON) turns a transcript into zero or more incidents. Each has a headline, a summary, a type, a category (breaking, notable or routine), the units involved and the location as spoken. Before deciding, it's shown the feed's incidents from the last 45 minutes, so follow-ups update an existing incident instead of starting a new one. Locations are geocoded with OpenStreetMap, limited to the feed's area; when there's no usable address, the marker sits at the feed's home and says "approx.".

Channel stories

Every saved transmission, triaged or not, also updates its channel's story: what that channel's current conversation is about. One AI call per transmission (openai/gpt-6-luna) gets the story so far and the new transcript, and returns:

  • same: whether this continues the story. It defaults to yes for replies, acknowledgements and garbled fragments. It says no only when a clearly different matter starts, or the current one has clearly ended.
  • subject: a headline of up to 8 words that stays put while the matter is worked, e.g. "Structure fire, Elm St". Routine chatter is one ongoing story, e.g. "Tower traffic, runway 14 in use".
  • facts: up to 6 labelled facts, rewritten as things change. For example units, command, size-up and status for a fire; vehicle, direction and last location for a pursuit.
  • note: one line for the chronology, or nothing when the transmission adds nothing.
  • alert (0–1) and why, used for notifications.

A story stays open while its channel has traffic within 30 minutes. The title line under the transport keys shows it; tap it for the story card.

The Stories tab, first on the printout, lists every story with traffic in the last 3 hours, latest first. Each row shows the channel, the subject, the time and count of its transmissions, "● live" if it had traffic in the last 5 minutes, and its newest transmission (its note for visitors without transcripts). Breaking stories print in red. New traffic moves its story to the top as it arrives. Tap a row for the story card.

The story card has the facts, then every transmission, newest first: its time, its chronology note and the full transcript, each playable. Transmissions that added nothing fold into "+ N quiet"; tap to open them in place. Ones that arrived while the card was open, or since you last opened it, are marked NEW.

Follow, on a Stories row or the story card, keeps that story on a slip under the radio: its two newest transmissions, with NEW on one that just arrived, and Open and Unfollow. One story at a time; following another replaces it. The choice is remembered in this browser. A story quiet for 2 hours says "quiet since" and stays until you unfollow it.

Closed captions

Transcripts arrive without word timings. So when a clip is first played, the server finds where in it the voice is (the same speech detection as the squelch) and spreads the transcript over those stretches, each line in proportion to its length. The result is a WebVTT file (/api/captions/<clip>.vtt), cached with the call, that the page attaches to the audio. That keeps the caption strip in step with play, pause and seek.

Tap anywhere on the strip for the story card of the transmission it shows (the newest line ends in Story › once its story is known; a scanned transmission can go on air a few seconds before that).

The strip rolls up three lines. Each transmission is one paragraph starting with its band tag and station, e.g. ATC MOB INTL. The text is monospace, so line breaks can be predicted: older text is trimmed from the front ("…") so every visible paragraph keeps its tag. When tuned live, the strip shows that channel's transcripts as they arrive, about a minute behind.

Scan

Scan plays recordings, not live streams: each new clip from the channels you've chosen, back to back, about a minute behind live. The page opens scanning. Browsers won't play sound before you touch the page, so the first transmission waits for a tap.

  • Channels. Scan follows whatever the Location knob and band keys show, minus stations you skip. When it starts, it queues up to 3 clips from the last 2 minutes so there's something to hear straight away.
  • Queue. New clips arrive over a server-sent event stream. Stations you put first go first, then oldest first. The queue keeps the newest 20, so Scan never falls far behind.
  • After each transmission Scan waits 2.5 s on that channel. A reply there, or traffic from a station you put first, plays at once, and you have time to press Hold.
  • Hold / Resume stays on one channel and plays its traffic as it arrives. It switches on once the first transmission has played.
  • Prev steps back through the channels Scan stopped on, one per press, and replays each one's last transmission.
  • Catch up plays the story so far of the channel on air (or just played); the key names that channel under its label, e.g. CATCH UP / MOB FIRE. It plays each transmission that added something, oldest first, then Scan carries on; until the first one is on air the title line says "Catching up · " and the story. The story card opens so you can read along, and its Play all plays every transmission the same way. Traffic from a station you put first still cuts in and the catch-up continues after it; other new traffic waits. While it runs the key reads Stop catch-up; press it to stop.
  • Wider catch-up, in the story card, also looks for the story on other channels: fire dispatch on one system and police on another for the same address, say. A model reads the story and the other channels' transmissions from the two hours before its latest one, picks the ones about the same incident, says why each is included, and sums up the whole incident; then it plays them all in order like Play all. Each look costs one model call (a fraction of a cent) and is kept until the story moves on. It's built from transcripts, so visitors who can't see transcripts don't get it.
  • First and skip. While scanning, tap a station once to put it first (FIRST: its traffic jumps the queue and interrupts other channels), again to skip it (SKIP: Scan leaves it out), again to reset. Your choices are saved in this browser.
  • Idle. With nothing queued, the needle sweeps across the scanned stations, like a scanner searching.

Why Scan never sticks on a noisy channel: it only plays clips that passed every stage above. A channel carrying only hiss, hum or an open mic never produces one: the adaptive squelch drops it before transcription, the filters drop invented text, and the speech gate drops filler. So there's nothing to stop on. A channel with real but dull chatter does still play; skip it or leave its band out.

Notifications

Standard Web Push, set up per browser with the bell on the grille. Each browser has its own sensitivity and favourite channels. Setup is in docs/NOTIFICATIONS.md.

  • When. The story's alert score × 1.4 for a favourite × what this browser has learned (×0.25–×2) has to reach the sensitivity: 0.8 Quiet, 0.6 Normal, 0.4 Chatty. A major event (0.9+) on a favourite always gets through.
  • How often. One notification per story. Another only if the story's score rises by 0.25 or more, and it replaces the earlier one. At most 6 an hour, except major events.
  • Learning. Each notification counts as evidence about its channel and story type:
    • More / Fewer: ±2.
    • Opened and stayed 60 s or more: +1 (15 s or more: +0.5).
    • Not opened within 30 minutes: −0.5.
    Evidence fades by half every 14 days. Ignoring alone can't push a weight below ×0.76; only Fewer reaches ×0.25.
  • Listener surges. Every 3 minutes the receiver reads each Broadcastify feed's listener count, minus its own recorder, and compares it with that feed's usual audience: the median for this hour and weekday over 4 weeks, falling back to shorter histories at first. At least 3× usual and +5 listeners (6 or more in all) triggers one closer-look AI call on the channel's story, which can raise its score. With nothing transcribed lately, a 5× surge of 10 or more becomes its own story.

Listening live

Tuning a station plays its live Broadcastify stream through the receiver (/listen/<feed>), which adds the Broadcastify login, so no sign-in box appears. Broadcastify only serves these streams to Premium accounts. On the LAN, and for owners, that's the receiver's own account. A guest signs in with their own Premium login, held in memory only, because passing the owner's subscription to others would be re-streaming.

The dial. The Look key picks how the dial is drawn, remembered in this browser: Channel bar, Preset keys, Tuning drum or Slide rule. Automatic uses the channel bar on a phone and the slide rule on a wide screen. The channel bar is one line for what's on air or tuned, over a lamp per channel grouped by band. A lamp is lit for 3 minutes after its channel's last transmission and blinks with each new one; it's ringed while on air or tuned, marked above when put first, and shows ⊘ when skipped. Tap the bar to open the preset keys, which tune and set First and Skip as usual.

Daily log

After midnight, each area's previous day is read back by openai/gpt-6-luna-pro. It picks the storylines worth retelling, prints them in the Daily log, and stitches their real audio into a highlight reel. "Today so far" builds one on request.

Studio

The owner's desk for turning light stories into short funny animations over the real radio. It's the Studio tab here, shown only to the owner (on the LAN, or signed in through Cloudflare Access); every Studio request from anyone else gets 403.

Suggestions. Stories from the last 48 hours pass a content guard first: never medical calls, anything alerting at 0.5 or more, or a story that mentions injuries, deaths, victims, arrests, weapons, minors, ambulances or hospitals; it also needs recordings left and enough said. The rest are scored by the triage model, 12 stories a call: humor (0–100), a pitch of at most 12 words, and safe, which is false when someone was hurt, arrested or mocked. Unsafe ones are kept out too; "Kept out by the content guard" lists every exclusion and why. Scores are kept until the story changes, so Refresh only pays for new and changed stories (about a cent for a whole 48 hours) and shows what it spent.

Make it. Pick a style: Sunday comic, Newspaper ink or Pixel art make a comic (MP4 + web page); Flat vector makes a cartoon (MP4 + web page). The estimate comes first, from the models' per-token prices; then the job queues. One job runs at a time, on the server, with its progress on the page:

  1. Audio: the story's clips in order, up to about 75 seconds of radio, with 0.6 s gaps.
  2. Script (anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5): title, captions per clip, three closing cards, and the panels or scene beats. Captions drop callsigns and unit numbers; no real names, likenesses, badges or house numbers. Any number of 3 or more digits is also stripped from every caption.
  3. Comic: 4–5 panels from google/gemini-2.5-flash-image (about $0.04 each; panel 1 is the style reference for the rest), then ffmpeg pans and zooms over them on a 4× source so the motion stays smooth, with captions, the clip's time and the closing cards. About $0.25 a comic.
  4. Cartoon: anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5 writes the scene code for an SVG animation; the page carries the audio inside it and draws every moment from one clock. It's checked in headless Chrome (it must draw all the way through without errors, and contain no outside URLs) and rewritten once if it fails, then stepped frame by frame at 20 fps into a 1080×1920 MP4. Without Chrome the cartoon is web-only. About $0.15–0.30.

The MP4 is the one to share: file viewers and messaging apps don't run a page's script. Web pages play in a sandbox with nothing from outside allowed in.

Money. Every call is metered (the Ledger lists them as Studio), and each animation records its models, calls and actual cost. STUDIO_DAILY_USD (default $2) caps a day's Studio spend: a job whose estimate would pass it isn't queued, and each paid call inside a job is checked again.

Library. Every animation, newest first, with its files in data/animations/<id>/ (never pruned): play, download, rate 1–5, delete. Animations made elsewhere can be added from the server's folder with node scripts/studio-import.mjs <dir> <meta.json>.

Sharing and privacy

The receiver runs at home. Visitors come through a Cloudflare Tunnel behind Cloudflare Access, so only invited email addresses get in. Raw transcripts can carry names, addresses and plates, so tunnel visitors don't get them unless the owner switches "Remote transcripts" on; incidents, stories and notifications never include them. Notification tracking (opened, time in view, More/Fewer) stays on this server, per browser.

Set up

Install and run

You need Node.js 22.13 or newer, ffmpeg and ffprobe, an OpenRouter API key, and a Broadcastify Premium account. Premium costs $15 for 6 months or $30 a year; a free account gets 401 from the stream server.

git clone https://github.com/iamneilroberts/squelch && cd squelch
npm install
cp .env.example .env
cp sources.example.json sources.json
npm start
  1. In .env, set OPENROUTER_API_KEY, BROADCASTIFY_LOGIN and BROADCASTIFY_PASSWORD. The login is your RadioReference/Broadcastify username, not your email.
  2. Describe your area in sources.json; see Find and add feeds.
  3. Open http://localhost:8077. Green lamps along the bottom mean a feed is being received.

No keys yet? examples/gulf-coast.json is a complete real setup, and scripts/seed-demo.mjs fills a demo database so the map and log have something to show. To keep it running after reboots on Linux, use the systemd units in docs/SHARING.md. To update: git pull && npm install, then restart.

Find and add feeds

  1. Find feeds. Open Broadcastify's directory for your state, then your county (broadcastify.com/listen/ctid/<county id>). Each feed shows an ID, what it covers, whether it's online, and how many people are listening. Prefer feeds that are online and have listeners.
  2. Check encryption. RadioReference lists each county's talkgroups. Encrypted police talkgroups aren't on any feed; fire and EMS usually are in the clear.
  3. Look beyond public safety. Airport tower and marine VHF feeds, amateur repeaters (SKYWARN nets) and rail feeds are all on Broadcastify. LiveATC and NOAA Weather Radio work as listen-only links.
  4. Add each feed to sources.json. The feed ID from the Broadcastify URL goes into both addresses: 
    { "id": "county-fire", "name": "County Fire Dispatch", "short": "CO FIRE", "dial": "P25",
  "band": "fire", "kind": "stream",
  "streamUrl": "https://audio.broadcastify.com/12345.mp3",
  "listenUrl": "https://www.broadcastify.com/listen/feed/12345",
  "coverage": "County fire and EMS dispatch; mutual aid with City FD.",
  "lat": 40.75, "lng": -73.98, "region": "home", "area": "My County, ST" }
    • band: one of police fire air marine rail weather ham. It picks the band key, the dial group and the caption tag.
    • kind: stream is recorded and transcribed; link is listen-only.
    • coverage: the triage and story models read it, so be specific.
    • id: never rename it once there's history.
    • Optional squelchDb (default −42): raise it toward −35 for a feed with a loud background.
  5. Regions are the Location knob positions. Each needs a map box (bbox: south, west, north, east), a weather point, an optional NOAA tide station, and a short vocab list of 20–50 local street and town names that help the transcriber. Keep the list certain: on a noisy clip, the transcriber may echo names it was given.
  6. Check it. Restart and watch for an hour. Green lamp = receiving; red = refused (check the login and feed ID); grey = listen-only. The Ledger's Speech detector panel shows how much each feed's squelch dropped. A feed that stays green but never produces a transcript may be dead air: remove it.

Every field is explained in docs/ADD-YOUR-REGION.md.

Turn on notifications

Once, on the receiver: run npm run vapid >> .env, set VAPID_SUBJECT to your email (mailto:) or site address (https://), and restart. Push only works on a secure page: https:// (a Cloudflare Tunnel or Tailscale Serve address, see Remote access) or http://localhost. A plain http://192.168… address won't do.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open the receiver in Safari.
  2. Share → Add to Home Screen. iOS only delivers web push to Home Screen apps (iOS 16.4+).
  3. Open it from the Home Screen icon.
  4. Tap the bell on the grille → Turn on → Allow.
  5. Send a test.

iPhone notifications have no buttons; give More/Fewer feedback on the story card that opens.

Android

  1. Open the receiver in Chrome (Firefox and Samsung Internet work too). No install needed.
  2. Tap the bell → Turn on → Allow.
  3. Send a test.
  4. Optional: Chrome menu ⋮ → Add to Home screen (or Install app) for an app window of its own.

Expand a notification for More like this / Fewer like this. If notifications arrive late or not at all, check Settings → Apps → Chrome → Notifications, and that battery saving isn't set to Restricted for Chrome.

Desktop

  1. Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari on macOS 13+: tap the bell → Turn on → Allow.
  2. Send a test.

On a Mac, also allow your browser in System Settings → Notifications. More/Fewer appear as buttons on the notification, or under its options menu, depending on the browser and system.

Then tune it: pick a sensitivity (Quiet, Normal or Chatty) and star favourite channels in the same card. Guests can try it only while the owner ticks Let guests turn on notifications; each guest's browser keeps its own settings. How notifications are chosen is under Notifications.

Remote access

Squelch is built to run at home. Never forward port 8077 on your router: anyone who reaches it directly is treated as being on your LAN, which means full owner access. Use one of these instead.

Cloudflare Tunnel + Access

Best for sharing with invited people. You need a domain on Cloudflare; Access is free for small teams.

  1. Install cloudflared.
  2. Zero Trust → Access → Applications → Add → Self-hosted: your hostname, a policy allowing the invited emails, login by one-time PIN. Do this first, so the site is never public.
  3. Zero Trust → Networks → Tunnels → Create: public hostname → http://127.0.0.1:8077. Save the token to a private file.
  4. cloudflared tunnel --no-autoupdate run --token-file /path/to/token
  5. Put your own email in OWNER_EMAILS in .env, so you keep owner powers when away from home.

Visitors get the map, incidents, stories and Scan. Raw transcripts appear only while the owner's Remote transcripts switch is on. To listen live, guests use their own Broadcastify Premium login.

Tailscale

Best for just you (and people you fully trust): a private network between your own devices, with no domain needed.

  1. Install Tailscale on the receiver and on your phone, signed in to the same account.
  2. In the Tailscale admin console, turn on MagicDNS and HTTPS certificates.
  3. On the receiver: tailscale serve --bg 8077.
  4. Open https://<machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net on your phone. It's HTTPS, so notifications work, including Add to Home Screen on iPhone.

Squelch treats Tailscale visitors like your LAN: full owner access, raw transcripts, and live audio on your Broadcastify account. So only share the machine with people you'd hand your receiver to. Don't use Tailscale Funnel, which makes it public.

Troubleshooting

A lamp is red
The stream refused the connection. Check BROADCASTIFY_LOGIN is your username, not your email; that the account is Premium; and the feed ID.
Green lamp, but no transcripts
The feed may be dead air or constant hum, which the squelch drops on purpose. The Ledger's Speech detector panel shows drops per feed.
"Receiver in standby"
No OPENROUTER_API_KEY. Add it and restart.
Tapping a station asks for a Broadcastify login
You're a guest: live audio needs your own Premium login. Owners and the LAN use the receiver's account.
No sound on Scan
Browsers won't play audio before you touch the page: tap anywhere once.
No bell on the grille
Guests only see it while the owner allows guest notifications.
Notifications don't arrive
Use Send a test in the bell's card. Check the page is HTTPS; on iPhone, that it was opened from the Home Screen; and that the OS allows notifications for the browser. If it still fails, turn notifications off and on again.
Wrong place on the map
Markers marked "approx." had no usable address and sit at the feed's home. Tighten the region's bbox and set each source's area.
Transcripts invent street names
Shorten the region's vocab to names you're sure of.
